UAE notice period pay calculator

Under Article 43 of the UAE Labour Law, either side ending a contract must give written notice of at least 30 and at most 90 days, and the worker is paid full wages during it. If a party does not serve the full notice, it owes the other side a notice-period allowance. This tool estimates that allowance for the days not served.

How it is calculated

Daily rate = last monthly wage ÷ days per month. Allowance = daily rate × unserved notice days. Example: wage AED 9,000, 30 days required, 10 days served. The daily rate is 9,000 ÷ 30 = AED 300, there are 20 unserved days, so the allowance is 20 × 300 = AED 6,000.

What Article 43 and the UAE Government Portal say

  • Notice must be in writing, between 30 and 90 days, and the contract stays in force until it ends.
  • The worker receives full wages during the notice period, based on the most recent wage.
  • A party that does not serve the full notice owes the other an allowance, even if no harm resulted.
  • When the employer ends the contract, the worker gets one unpaid day per week to look for a new job, declared at least 3 days in advance.

Notice rules during probation are described differently across official pages, so this tool does not model them. See our probation guide for what we could confirm.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the notice period in the UAE?

Between 30 and 90 days under Article 43 of Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021. The exact length is in your contract.

Am I paid during my notice period?

Yes. The UAE Government Portal says the worker receives the full wage during the notice period.

What if I leave without serving notice?

A party that does not serve the full notice owes the other a notice-period allowance. For a resignation, an employee who leaves early to join another employer may also face a work-permit ban if it falls in probation; see resignation vs termination.
