How gratuity is calculated in the UAE

End-of-service gratuity is a lump sum your employer pays when your employment ends, as long as you completed at least one year of continuous service. For private-sector expatriates it is set by Article 51 of Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021 (the UAE Labour Law). The formula has not changed under the current law:

Length of serviceGratuity per year
Less than 1 yearNothing
1 to 5 years21 days of basic salary per year
More than 5 years21 days per year for the first 5 years, then 30 days per year for the rest

Step-by-step example

Suppose your basic salary is AED 6,000 per month and you worked 7 years and 3 months:

  1. Daily basic wage = 6,000 × 12 ÷ 365 = AED 197.26
  2. First 5 years: 5 × 21 = 105 days × 197.26 = AED 20,712.33
  3. Remaining 2.25 years: 2.25 × 30 = 67.5 days × 197.26 = AED 13,315.07
  4. Total gratuity = AED 34,027.40

Part-years count pro rata once you pass one full year. The total is capped at two years' wages - AED 144,000 in this example, so the cap rarely bites before roughly 21 years of service.

Rules people often miss

  • Basic salary only. Housing, transport, utilities and other allowances are excluded. Two people with the same total package can get very different gratuities if their basic shares differ.
  • Unpaid absence does not count. Days of absence without pay are subtracted from your service period before the calculation.
  • Resigning no longer costs you gratuity. Under the old law, resigning from an unlimited contract before 5 years reduced the payout. That system ended in February 2022 - see limited vs unlimited contracts.
  • Your employer must pay within 14 days of the contract ending, together with any outstanding wages, and may deduct amounts you owe the company.
  • Part-time and other work models get gratuity pro-rated by working hours (Cabinet Resolution No. 1 of 2022, Article 30).
  • DIFC is different. The Dubai International Financial Centre replaced gratuity with the DEWS workplace savings scheme. Some other free zones have their own arrangements.
  • UAE nationals are covered by the GPSSA pension system instead of gratuity.

The optional Savings Scheme

MOHRE now offers employers a voluntary alternative: instead of accruing gratuity, the employer pays a monthly contribution (5.83% of basic salary under 5 years of service, 8.33% above) into an approved investment fund, and the employee receives the contributions plus returns. If your employer joined, gratuity already accrued before enrolment must still be settled under the traditional formula.
