UAE VAT registration threshold: mandatory and voluntary

You do not need to charge VAT from the first dirham you earn, but once your business passes a set turnover you must register with the Federal Tax Authority (FTA). The thresholds are AED 375,000 for mandatory registration and AED 187,500 for voluntary registration. This guide explains how each test works, with examples, and what to do next.

Mandatory registration: AED 375,000

The FTA says a business must register for VAT if the total value of its taxable supplies and imports exceeded AED 375,000 over the previous 12 months, or it expects its taxable supplies and imports to exceed that amount in the next 30 days. The threshold does not apply to foreign businesses, which are dealt with separately by the FTA.

"Taxable supplies" means supplies that carry VAT at 5% or at zero. Exempt supplies are not counted. If you are unsure how your sales are classified, read the FTA's VAT guides before deciding.

Voluntary registration: AED 187,500

If you do not meet the mandatory test, you may still register if your taxable supplies and imports, or your taxable expenses, exceeded AED 187,500 over the previous 12 months, or you expect them to in the next 30 days. This helps start-ups that have large setup costs on which they want to recover VAT, and businesses whose customers require a VAT number on invoices.

The 30-day forward-looking test

The "next 30 days" limb means you cannot wait for a year of history. If you win a contract worth more than the threshold that will be delivered within 30 days, you may need to register straight away. The FTA's wording is about what you expect to exceed in the next 30 days, so keep contracts and projections that show how you assessed it.

After you cross the threshold

A business that is required to register must submit its application to the FTA; the FTA's terms and conditions give a 30-day period from the date the obligation arises, which we have flagged for your own check. Register through the FTA's online portal and keep the evidence you used to calculate turnover.

What changes once registered?

  • You charge 5% VAT on standard-rated supplies and 0% on zero-rated ones; try the VAT invoice calculator.
  • You issue tax invoices with the required details (see the FTA's VAT guide).
  • You file VAT returns and may recover input VAT on business costs.
  • Record-keeping duties apply.

If you are below the threshold

You do not charge VAT and cannot recover VAT on your costs. Monitor your turnover each month: add the last 12 months' taxable supplies and imports, and compare with the threshold. A spreadsheet row per month is enough. Re-check whenever you sign a large contract.

Tracking your rolling 12 months

Because the test looks back over the previous 12 months, a simple monthly log works better than an annual figure. List taxable sales (and imports) for each month; the sum of the latest 12 is your running total. Example for a small agency:

MonthTaxable sales (AED)Running 12-month total
Oct Y138,000295,000
Nov Y140,000335,000
Dec Y142,000377,000 (over threshold)
Jan Y245,000404,000 (over threshold)
Feb Y248,000430,000 (over threshold)

The agency's rolling total passes AED 375,000 in the last row shown, so the mandatory registration obligation arises at that point (and earlier if it had expected to pass it within the next 30 days). The point of the table is the method: look at the latest 12 months, not the calendar year, and look forward too.

Records to keep

  • Monthly sales and import records that show which supplies were taxable, zero-rated or exempt.
  • Contracts and quotations supporting your 30-day expectation.
  • A dated note of when you assessed the threshold and what you concluded.
  • Invoices and receipts for taxable expenses if you apply voluntarily on that basis.

Common mistakes

  1. Only checking the previous calendar year and ignoring the rolling 12 months.
  2. Forgetting the 30-day forward-looking test.
  3. Counting exempt sales as taxable (or the reverse).
  4. Assuming a freelancer or home business is outside VAT; the thresholds apply to the business, not its size or location.

Frequently asked questions

What is the VAT registration threshold in the UAE?

AED 375,000 of taxable supplies and imports for mandatory registration, and AED 187,500 for voluntary registration, per the Federal Tax Authority.

Do I count the last 12 months or the calendar year?

The FTA test looks at the previous 12 months, plus an expectation for the next 30 days.

Does the threshold apply to foreign businesses?

No. The FTA says the mandatory threshold does not apply to foreign businesses, which are treated separately.

What is the VAT rate?

The standard rate is 5%, per the Federal Tax Authority.
