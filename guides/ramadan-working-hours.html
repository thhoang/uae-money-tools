Working hours in Ramadan for UAE private-sector employees

During the holy month of Ramadan, working hours in the UAE private sector are reduced by two hours per day. This guide explains how that sits on top of the normal working-hours rules, how it affects weekly totals, what the Ministry (MOHRE) has said about flexible and remote work, and which questions the published sources do not answer.

The normal rules first

Article 17 of Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021 sets normal private-sector working hours at 8 hours per day or 48 hours per week. Commuting from home to work is not counted as working time, except for certain worker categories. After five consecutive hours of work, the employee is entitled to one or more breaks of no less than one hour in total; breaks do not count as working time.

The Ramadan reduction

The UAE Government Portal states that in Ramadan working hours are reduced by 2 hours per day. MOHRE announces this each year; its notice dated 12 February 2026, ahead of Ramadan 2026, carries the headline "Reduction of Working Hours for Private Sector Employees by Two Hours Daily During the Holy Month of Ramadan". The same notice says private-sector companies may use flexible working arrangements or remote work within the limits of the daily working hours set for the days of Ramadan.

If your contract already sets shorter normal hours than the legal maximum, ask your employer how the reduction applies to your schedule; the sources we reviewed state the reduction but do not give a worked example for shorter contracts.

Overtime in Ramadan

Outside Ramadan, overtime is paid at the normal hourly rate (based on basic salary) plus 25%, or plus 50% between 10 pm and 4 am, with a limit of 2 extra hours per day. Shift workers are excluded from these rates. The pages we reviewed do not say whether time worked beyond the reduced Ramadan schedule is treated as overtime, or what the reference "normal hours" are. This is a point to raise with your employer or MOHRE if it matters to you. You can model both views in the overtime pay calculator.

Illustration. Basic AED 6,000, 30 days, 8 hours: the normal hourly rate is AED 25.00. One extra hour at +25% is AED 31.25. If your employer treats the sixth hour as part of a reduced schedule, working a seventh hour could be extra time; whether and at what rate it is paid should be confirmed with them.

Is pay reduced when hours are reduced?

The sources we reviewed describe a reduction of working hours, not of pay, and none says wages may be cut. We do not give a conclusion beyond that: if your employer proposes to change your pay in Ramadan, ask for the legal basis in writing and contact MOHRE.

Summer midday work ban (a different rule)

Do not confuse Ramadan reductions with the summer midday break. Under Ministerial Resolution No. 44 of 2022, outdoor work directly under the sun is not allowed between 12:30 pm and 3:00 pm from 15 June to 15 September. It is a safety rule that applies on its own timetable.

Practical tips for employees

  1. Ask HR for the Ramadan timetable in writing before the month starts.
  2. Check how breaks are scheduled; breaks do not count as working hours.
  3. Keep a record of any extra hours, with dates and who requested them.
  4. If you work remotely, agree specific working hours with your employer.

Practical tips for employers

Publish the Ramadan schedule early, apply the reduction consistently to all private-sector staff, keep timesheets so any extra hours are provable and make sure managers know the 2-hour daily overtime cap still applies.

Frequently asked questions

How many hours do you work in Ramadan in the UAE?

Normal hours are reduced by two hours per day, so a standard 8-hour day becomes 6 hours, according to the UAE Government Portal.

Does the Ramadan reduction apply to the whole private sector?

MOHRE announces the reduction for private-sector employees. Sector or contract-specific questions should be confirmed with MOHRE.

Can employers use remote work in Ramadan?

MOHRE's 2026 notice says companies may implement flexible working arrangements or remote work within the limits of the daily working hours set for Ramadan.

How much is overtime pay?

The normal hourly rate based on basic salary plus 25% (plus 50% between 10 pm and 4 am), excluding shift workers.
